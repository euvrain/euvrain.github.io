The Problem

Running All The Paws LLC as a real pet services business, I noticed something: I was making scheduling and pricing decisions based on intuition alone. When demand spiked, I didn't know until it was too late to staff up. When demand was low, I was leaving pricing on the table by charging flat rates regardless of conditions.

I framed this as two distinct ML problems — demand forecasting and dynamic pricing — and built a full-stack production system to solve both.

Approach
01

Exploratory Data Analysis

Performed EDA across 9,935 NJ booking records, profiling demand patterns by time of day, day of week, location, and walker. Identified clear peak windows and geographic clusters that informed feature engineering.

02

Demand Forecasting Model

Trained a GradientBoosting model on time-series folds to predict booking volume by time slot. Used 5-fold cross-validation targeting MAE as the primary operational metric. Achieved 5% RMSE improvement over baseline with stable cross-validation MAE of 0.774 ± 0.033.

03

Dynamic Pricing Model

Built a Ridge Regression model predicting surge multipliers per booking based on demand signals. Regularization chosen deliberately to prevent overfitting while maintaining interpretability. R²=0.727, producing 16.7% simulated revenue lift over flat-rate baseline across 1,856 held-out test cases.

04

Production Deployment

Both models wrapped in a FastAPI backend exposing live REST endpoints. React operator dashboard visualizes demand heatmaps, surge pricing schedules, and walker performance across 8 active walkers. Frontend deployed on Vercel, backend on Railway.

Results

The platform transforms scheduling and pricing from intuition-based decisions into data-driven ones. The demand forecasting model enables proactive staffing decisions; the dynamic pricing engine captures revenue that flat-rate pricing leaves behind.

The React dashboard puts both models in the hands of a non-technical operator — real-time heatmaps, surge schedules, and walker metrics all in one view.
