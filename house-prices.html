The Problem

Given a residential housing dataset with 79 features, predict sale prices as accurately as possible. The challenge: most of the 79 features add noise rather than signal. The goal was to build a reproducible pipeline that goes from raw CSV to final predictions, with careful feature selection and model comparison along the way.

The secondary goal was to interpret model outputs in a way that's useful to a non-technical audience — identifying which factors actually drive housing value.

Approach
01

Exploratory Data Analysis

Profiled all 79 features for distributions, missing values, and correlations with sale price. Identified skewed variables, categorical encodings needed, and potential multicollinearity issues.

02

Feature Engineering

Used Random Forest feature importance to identify the top predictive variables. Engineered 5 new predictive features, reducing the feature space from 79 to a focused set that improved model signal and reduced noise.

03

Model Training & Comparison

Trained and compared three models on 1,460 samples. Tuned hyperparameters (n_estimators, max_depth) via cross-validation. Random Forest won on both R² and RMSE.

04

Interpretation

Translated regression coefficients and feature importances into plain-language findings: lot size, neighborhood, and build quality are the top three drivers of housing value. Results framed for a non-technical audience.

Key Findings

The top three drivers of housing sale price in this dataset:

1. Lot size — larger lots command significantly higher prices
2. Neighborhood — location remains the strongest non-structural predictor
3. Build quality — overall material and finish grade directly impacts value

The fully reproducible pipeline runs from raw Kaggle CSV to final predictions with documented methodology and assumptions at every step.
